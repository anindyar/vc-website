DNS zones accrete like sediment. The record that points at a host decommissioned two years ago. The TTL someone set to 24 hours "temporarily" during an outage — in 2019. The split-horizon zone where internal and external copies drifted apart because two teams edit them. The forwarder chain that quietly loops back on itself under load. Whether you run BIND, CoreDNS, PowerDNS, Unbound, dnsmasq, Windows DNS, or cloud-hosted zones — VibeComputing handles the full spectrum of DNS operations: zone and record audits, drift detection, stale-record sweeps, split-horizon consistency checks, forwarding-path analysis, DNSSEC validation review, and change propagation planning. Stop discovering the bad record during the outage it caused.

The agent connects in seconds and maps the resolution path the way a senior engineer would — which queries succeed internally but NXDOMAIN externally, which records point at hosts that no longer exist, which secondary is serving a serial from last month, which resolver forwards to a forwarder that forwards back, what the TTL math says about when your fix actually lands everywhere. "Why can't the branch reach the payments API?" "Which records point at dead hosts?" "When will this change propagate?" The AI correlates zone files, resolver configs, query paths, and infrastructure state into a single picture — and separates the split-horizon drift from the cached negative answer from the genuinely down backend, instead of making you dig +trace at 2 AM.

For network teams that live under change-control, VibeComputing fits existing setups without ceremony. The zero-trust outbound-only agent model works inside locked-down environments — no inbound ports, no resolver admin interfaces exposed to the internet. Read-only by default: the AI analyzes zones, records, and query paths, and every record edit, zone reload, or forwarder change is shown as the exact command before it runs. Hostnames, IPs, and zone names are obfuscated before they reach any model. Humans approve every mutation. Combined with BYOK for strict control over your AI provider and air-gapped deployment for regulated environments, it's the safest way to manage DNS with AI.

Example:

$ why can't anyone at the office reach the payments API?

→[OBFUSCATING] Masking hostnames, IPs, and zone names...

→ Public resolvers: [masked-host] → NXDOMAIN. Internal resolver: resolves fine → split-horizon zone

→ Zone drift: external zone updated 6 days ago; internal copy still serves the OLD address — zones owned by two different teams

→ Stale TTL: 14,400s on the drifted record — branch caches will hold the bad answer up to 4h after the fix

→ Side findings: 7 records across 3 zones → decommissioned host [masked-oldsrv]; forwarder loop risk on [masked-res-1] (A→B→A fallback path); secondary serial format mismatch

Verdict: correct the internal zone copy, drop TTL to 300s 24h before the next window, then cut over (exact commands attached). Awaiting your approval.
